As the semester moved closer to its conclusion, the evaluation phase of our major projects officially began. Our **Database Systems Project**, which had been divided into three stages by our instructor, was finally ready for assessment. As mentioned in my previous articles, the project required us to first select and approve a dataset, then design a database based on that dataset, apply Machine Learning techniques for prediction and finally develop a frontend using FastAPI. After completing all these stages and following the provided guidelines, we submitted our project before the deadline. Since Friday was an official holiday, our database lab sessions were conducted online. Our instructor scheduled the project evaluations through Google Classroom, where students shared their screens, explained their code and answered technical questions related to their work. Alhamdulillah, our evaluation went smoothly and many students completed their assessments on the same day. Those who could not be evaluated were scheduled for the following week. In addition, we were informed that the project would also be presented in an upcoming exhibition where faculty members from different departments could ask questions about our work. This encouraged all students to thoroughly understand every aspect of their projects. After the evaluation, we submitted the complete project in the form of a compressed ZIP file before the final deadline.

While the Database Project evaluation was taking place, our **Object-Oriented Programming (OOP) Project** was also entering its final stage. As I mentioned in my previous article, I had developed an **Endless Car Lane Runner** game using Unity and C#. During the project presentation session, our instructor asked each student to present their project as if they were pitching a product to potential customers. Instead of only explaining technical details, we were expected to discuss the business value, target audience and practical benefits of our projects. When my turn came, I explained the features of my game, the user-friendly design and how it could serve as an enjoyable offline entertainment option, especially for students and travelers. I highlighted the scoring system, engaging gameplay and simple controls that made the game accessible to a wide range of users. Alhamdulillah, my presentation was well received, and I achieved full marks in the project evaluation. This achievement was particularly meaningful because I had invested significant effort into learning Unity, solving technical challenges and developing the project from scratch. Around the same time, another proud moment came when our instructor selected my OOP Lab Manual as one of the best manuals in the entire class. Out of all the students, only two manuals were chosen and mine was one of them. The instructor appreciated the quality of my code documentation, formatting, explanations and organized presentation. The class later decided to print and bind my manual as a reference document. This reminded me of a similar achievement during my first semester when my ICT Lab Manual was selected as an example for junior students. Such moments motivated me to continue maintaining high standards in my academic work.

Alongside these achievements, we also had to prepare for our **Digital Logic Design (DLD) Open-Ended Lab Evaluation**. A few days before the assessment, our instructor shared detailed instructions in the class group and advised us to revise all previous lab manuals, circuit diagrams, IC numbers and logic gate concepts. He also provided a helpful website that contained learning resources related to the course. Understanding the importance of this evaluation, I dedicated a significant amount of time to preparation. I carefully reviewed every circuit diagram, practiced their implementation and used simulation software to test and understand the circuits before applying them on a breadboard. In fact, I spent most of the night before the evaluation revising concepts and performing practical exercises. The next day, during the open-ended lab, we were given a scenario-based problem that required both theoretical understanding and practical implementation. Thanks to thorough preparation, I was able to complete the quiz portion within the first fifteen minutes and then immediately begin the hardware implementation with my group. Alhamdulillah, our group successfully completed the task and secured **full marks (5 out of 5)** in the evaluation. This experience reinforced the importance of preparation, teamwork and practical learning. Looking back, this period of the semester was filled with evaluations, achievements and valuable learning experiences that strengthened both my technical knowledge and my confidence as a Computer Engineering student.
