After students raised concerns regarding the internet connectivity issues on campus, the university management responded positively to our application and assured us that the problem was being addressed. They informed us that a technical issue was causing the disruptions and that efforts were underway to resolve it as quickly as possible. Alhamdulillah, within the next two to three days, the situation improved significantly. Although occasional interruptions still occurred, the internet was no longer unavailable for long periods. With this issue largely under control, our attention shifted back to academics, as two important quizzes were approaching. The first quiz was for Object-Oriented Programming (OOP), where our instructor announced that the assessment would consist of twenty multiple-choice questions covering the complete theoretical portion of the course. The purpose of this quiz was to evaluate how well students understood the fundamental concepts taught throughout the semester. Fortunately, our instructor had explained every topic in a detailed and organized manner, making it easier for me to revise and prepare confidently for the quiz.

At the same time, our Digital Logic Design (DLD) instructor, Sir Abdullah Bilal, also scheduled a theory quiz from **Chapter 4: Combinational Logic**. Along with the quiz announcement, he assigned a group-based theoretical report. The groups were the same as those formed for DLD lab activities, consisting of three students each. We were required to prepare a five-page report on a topic that would be assigned by the instructor. One of the most important conditions was that the report had to be written entirely by students without using AI tools or external writing assistance. Furthermore, we were instructed to verify the report using a professional plagiarism and AI-detection platform and attach the verification report with our submission. This assignment encouraged us to conduct genuine research, improve our technical writing skills and maintain academic integrity. As a result, my group started planning early and discussed how we could divide responsibilities efficiently while ensuring the quality of our work.

Meanwhile, our Communication Skills course became much more interactive after the midterm examinations. Before the mids, most of our lessons focused on theoretical concepts such as the **7 Cs of Communication** and other communication principles. However, after the examinations, our instructor shifted towards practical learning activities. One such assignment required every student to prepare and deliver an individual presentation. We had to select a topic related to any academic subject, create PowerPoint slides, deliver a five-minute presentation and then spend another five minutes interacting with classmates by answering questions. Since the entire activity had to be conducted in English, I spent my weekend preparing carefully. I selected **Wind Energy** as my topic because I had previously worked on a renewable energy project at the college level and was already familiar with the subject. I designed my slides, practiced multiple times and prepared myself thoroughly. Although I felt slightly nervous at the beginning while standing in front of the class, my preparation and confidence helped me perform well. Alhamdulillah, the presentation was successful and I secured **9 out of 10 marks**. This experience not only improved my public speaking skills but also increased my confidence in communicating technical concepts effectively in front of an audience.
