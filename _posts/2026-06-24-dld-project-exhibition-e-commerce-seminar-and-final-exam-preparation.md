As the semester approached its final stage, we had to complete and submit our **Digital Logic Design (DLD) Assignment**. As mentioned in my previous articles, our instructor had assigned each group a topic from **Chapter 4: Combinational Logic** and required us to prepare a detailed report completely on our own. My group was assigned the topic **"Multiplexer and Demultiplexer."** The report included definitions, circuit diagrams, truth tables, working principles, and real-life applications of both concepts. One of the most important requirements was that the report had to remain within the plagiarism and AI limits defined by our instructor. Therefore, my group members and I carefully researched the topic, wrote the report ourselves, and reviewed every section to ensure originality. After completing the assignment, we used a professional plagiarism and AI-detection service to verify the document. Alhamdulillah, our report achieved **0% AI-generated content** and only **11% plagiarism**, which was well within the allowed limit. After completing all requirements, we submitted the assignment online before the deadline. Around the same time, our **DLD Project Exhibition** also arrived. Our project, **Digital ATM PIN Verification System**, was already completed, and on the night before the exhibition, we finalized its model and presentation materials. The next day, students arrived with their projects, and the exhibition officially began. Our instructor, Sir Abdullah Bilal, visited every project, reviewed reports, asked technical questions, and evaluated students based on their understanding of the project. Later, teachers from other departments and senior students also visited our stall, appreciated our work, and discussed the practical implementation of the project. For me, this was one of the most valuable learning experiences of the semester because it strengthened my understanding of hardware design, logic circuits, and real-world implementation.

During the same day, an interesting **E-Commerce Seminar** was organized by the IEEE student community for Computer Engineering and Electrical Engineering students. Since our final examinations were approaching and summer vacations were just around the corner, the purpose of the seminar was to guide students on how they could utilize their free time productively. The guest speaker was **Mr. Talha Yousaf**, an experienced professional in the field of E-Commerce who had recently completed his graduation and had been working in this industry since his student life. The seminar began with an interactive discussion where students were asked questions about E-Commerce and online business opportunities. Since I had some prior interest in freelancing and online earning platforms, I actively participated in the discussion and answered several questions. Mr. Talha then shared his personal journey, challenges, achievements, and practical strategies that helped him build his career. He introduced different platforms, explained how beginners can start learning, and discussed the skills required to succeed in the modern digital marketplace. The seminar was highly motivating and gave students a broader perspective about career opportunities beyond traditional academic pathways. Personally, I found the session very inspiring because I had already been interested in freelancing and online business. Listening to his experiences encouraged me to consider utilizing my upcoming summer vacation to explore this field further and develop additional professional skills alongside my academic journey.

Shortly after the seminar, the university officially shared our **Final Examination Date Sheet**, marking the beginning of the most important phase of the semester. According to the schedule, our examinations were set to begin on **15 June 2026** and conclude on **22 June 2026**. As students reviewed the date sheet, the atmosphere across the campus changed noticeably. Most projects, assignments, lab activities, and presentations had already been completed, leaving only examinations and final results ahead. Our teachers encouraged us to focus entirely on our studies, reminding us that these final days would play an important role in determining our semester performance. Their advice motivated many students, including myself, to organize revision plans and prepare seriously for the upcoming exams. Although grades and CGPA are important, I have always believed that practical skills and continuous learning are equally valuable. Throughout the second semester, I worked hard not only to achieve good academic results but also to gain hands-on experience through projects, presentations, exhibitions, and technical activities. Looking back, this semester taught me countless lessons, helped me develop new skills, and provided opportunities to grow both academically and personally. With the final examinations approaching, I felt grateful for everything I had learned and hopeful that the effort I invested throughout the semester would produce positive results.
