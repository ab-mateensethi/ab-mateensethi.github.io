Finally, the moment I had been waiting for arrived and my summer holidays came to an end. It was my last day at home after almost two and a half months of holidays and the next day I was going to return to university for the beginning of my third semester. I was excited because I was ready to learn new things, meet my friends again and begin another chapter of my university journey, but at the same time, I felt a little emotional because I knew I would miss the life I had enjoyed during these holidays. I had spent quality time with my family and friends, visited my village, worked on different courses and skills and made my holidays both enjoyable and productive. I knew that after returning to university, my routine would become busy again with daily classes, assignments, projects and responsibilities, while being away from my family would once again teach me to become more independent. On my last evening at home, I prepared my luggage and made sure everything I needed for university was packed. The next morning, I met my family, took my parents' prayers and blessings, remembered Allah and prayed for a successful new journey before leaving for university. When I finally reached the university and stood outside the gate with my luggage, I experienced a strong feeling of nostalgia. Around one year earlier, I had entered through the same gate as a completely inexperienced student who knew very little about university life. Now, one year later, I was standing there with a completely different mindset. I felt proud that during this first year I had learned not only through my courses and academic projects but also through teamwork, communication, leadership, practical experiences and the challenges of student life. I had developed new skills, learned from my mistakes and brought many positive changes to myself. With this realization, I entered the university once again with the name of Allah and met my old friends. I also welcomed the new juniors who had joined our campus, settled my luggage in my room, cleaned and organized my space and prepared myself for another journey. The next day, I went to the department and met our teachers and classmates. It felt great to see everyone again because I had genuinely missed the university environment. We discussed our holidays, shared what we had learned, talked about our experiences and caught up with one another. Since the teachers were also busy during the first week preparing schedules and arranging lectures for the newly admitted juniors, the first few days were relatively normal. We attended our classes, met some new teachers and introduced ourselves to them. Our new teachers asked about our backgrounds and interests, while we also learned about their teaching methods. Our previous teachers asked about our results, GPA and how we had spent our holidays. By the third day, we received our new timetable and seeing the subjects of the third semester made me even more excited. I already knew the subject list, but now that the semester had officially started, the new syllabus, new topics and especially the new programming course gave me a fresh motivation to learn and improve.

As the first week progressed, my university life became connected with another important responsibility that I had received during the holidays. Since I had joined the IEEE CIS team as the Event Management Head, I attended my first physical meetings with the society after previously completing the registration and interview process online. During these meetings, all the members introduced themselves, we became familiar with one another and different responsibilities were discussed. One of our first major tasks was to plan an orientation for the newly admitted juniors. As members of the CE25 batch, we worked together to organize the orientation and make the new students feel comfortable in their new university environment. We also interacted with the juniors personally and guided them wherever needed. On the fifth day of the first week, the orientation event was organized successfully. I participated in the event along with my friends as a member of the society and we arranged different games and quiz sessions to keep the juniors engaged. I also had the opportunity to speak with them about professionalism and student life. It was a very enjoyable experience because I was now experiencing university life from a different position—not only as a senior student but also as someone responsible for organizing activities and helping others. After the first week, academic life started becoming more regular and during the second week another major opportunity came through IEEE. A large event called the "STLC — IEEE ComSoc Student Leadership Conference 2026" was organized, with our UET Faisalabad Campus serving as the organizer. The event brought together members and representatives from different IEEE branches and societies, including the relevant student leadership and executive members. Before the event, several meetings were held where everyone was informed about the importance of the event, their individual roles, responsibilities and the rules and procedures they needed to follow. We were also guided on how to welcome guests, manage participants and perform our assigned duties professionally. The main event was held at "Chiniot Palace Banquets in Faisalabad" and we reached the venue around 9:00 AM. Before the event officially began, we had a short team meeting, after which everyone moved to their assigned responsibilities. My responsibility was to welcome guests and guide them to their seats and I tried my best to perform this duty professionally and respectfully. The event was especially valuable because professionals, industry representatives and experienced speakers from different fields were invited to interact with students. They discussed technology, future communication networks, including developments related to 5G and 6G and shared knowledge from their professional experiences. Although I attended the event as part of my assigned responsibility, I also considered it a learning opportunity. I listened carefully, interacted with people and tried to take something valuable from every session. The event ended with refreshments, guest feedback, group photographs and many memorable moments. For me, it was more than simply attending another university event; it was another practical experience that helped me understand event management, teamwork, professional communication and responsibility in a real environment.

After these activities, my regular university life continued and I also started implementing something I had planned during my summer holidays. One of the most useful seminars I had attended during the break was the GitHub session, which had taught me how to make my GitHub presence more professional and organize my projects properly. I decided that I should not leave my previous work scattered across different places. Instead, I wanted to create a proper digital record of my learning journey. I had already completed many projects during my first and second semesters, including university assignments and projects, along with several personal and learning-based projects that I had created to improve my skills. Therefore, I started uploading these projects to GitHub and organizing them through proper repositories. For each project, I created a new repository and used Git Bash to push the project files to GitHub. This process helped me understand Git and GitHub more practically because I was not simply watching tutorials anymore; I was applying the concepts to my own work. It also gave me a sense of security because keeping my projects in organized repositories meant that I would have an online backup of my work and could access my projects again in the future if something happened to my local files. More importantly, I started looking at GitHub not just as a place to store code, but as part of my professional digital identity. I wanted my GitHub profile to show my university projects, learning projects, technical progress and the skills I was developing throughout my degree. You can visit my GitHub profile here: [github.com/ab-mateensethi](https://github.com/ab-mateensethi). My goal was to gradually build a portfolio that could show what I had learned throughout my university journey and how my skills were developing semester by semester. Looking back, the beginning of my third semester was therefore much more than simply returning to classes. I returned with new subjects, new responsibilities, new juniors to guide, an important position in IEEE CIS, practical event-management experience and a stronger focus on building my professional presence. The first year had taught me where I was starting from, while the beginning of the third semester gave me a clearer idea of where I wanted to go. With Allah's guidance, I was ready to continue learning, take responsibility, build stronger skills and make this new chapter of my university life even more meaningful.
