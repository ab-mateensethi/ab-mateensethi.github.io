After receiving approval for our **Heart Failure Prediction** dataset, my group partner and I officially started working on our Database Systems project. Our instructor had already explained the complete roadmap of the project and divided it into three major phases. The first phase was selecting and getting approval for a suitable dataset, which we had successfully completed. The second phase involved designing a database and applying Machine Learning techniques to generate predictions, while the final phase required developing a frontend using FastAPI. Knowing that project deadlines from other subjects would also arrive soon, we decided to begin our work early and progress step by step. During the first week, we focused on understanding the dataset and successfully completed the database design. This early start gave us confidence and helped us manage our workload more effectively.

At the same time, our group also began working on the **Digital Logic Design (DLD) Lab Project**, which was a Digital ATM PIN Verification System using Integrated Circuits (ICs). Compared to other assignments, this project was more challenging and required a significant amount of practical work. We searched extensively on YouTube and other online resources but could not find a complete guide that matched our requirements. Therefore, we decided to seek help from senior students in the Electrical Engineering department, who had already studied similar concepts. Fortunately, one senior guided us through the fundamentals of the project and explained how to approach the circuit design. Even then, we faced several difficulties during implementation. At one point, a breadboard was damaged and some resistors stopped working, forcing us to purchase replacements from the market. Despite these setbacks, we remained committed to completing the project on time. With the support of our seniors, careful wire-to-wire connections and continuous testing, we gradually made progress and learned valuable practical skills along the way.

While we were busy with our projects, another exciting academic moment arrived. Our Object-Oriented Programming instructor, Dr. Akram, informed us that our midterm papers would be discussed and returned during the next class. Since the OOP paper had been relatively difficult, many students were anxious about their results. Although I was hopeful because my exam had gone well, I still prayed for a good outcome. The next day, when the papers were distributed, I was extremely happy and grateful to learn that I had secured **28 out of 30 marks**. This achievement became even more special when I found out that these were the highest marks among both Section A and Section B. Considering that the overall class average was around 18-19 marks, this result gave me a great sense of accomplishment and motivated me to work even harder. Looking back, this week was a combination of project development, problem-solving, teamwork and academic success, all of which contributed positively to my growth as a Computer Engineering student.
