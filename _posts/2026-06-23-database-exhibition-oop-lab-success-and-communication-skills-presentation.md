As the semester continued towards its final stage, the date for our Database Systems Project Exhibition was officially announced. One day before the event, our instructor shared detailed instructions with all students and explained how the exhibition would be organized. Each group was assigned a table where they had to present their project professionally, and all groups were instructed to arrange themselves according to their roll numbers. The exhibition started at 9:00 AM, and students, faculty members and senior students visited the project stalls throughout the event. Besides presenting my project, I was also given an additional responsibility. As I mentioned in previous articles, I had helped establish a Computer Engineering community for our department, and because of my experience in designing digital content, I was recommended to create the official exhibition poster and standee. I designed both materials carefully, focusing on a clean, professional and attractive appearance. After printing them, they were displayed outside our department building, and many students appreciated the design and presentation. During the exhibition, several senior students and teachers visited our stall and asked questions about our project. One of the most common questions was why we had integrated Machine Learning into a Database Systems Project. We explained that databases are primarily responsible for storing, organizing and retrieving data, while Machine Learning helps analyze that data and generate predictions. We then demonstrated how we designed the database, trained the prediction model and connected it with a frontend developed using HTML, JavaScript and FastAPI. Alhamdulillah, our group presented the project confidently, received positive feedback and our instructor appreciated the quality and uniqueness of our work.

Following the project exhibition, another important academic activity was our Object-Oriented Programming (OOP) Open-Ended Lab Evaluation. This was the final lab assessment before the completion of the course. At the start of the session, our instructor asked all students to use the university computers instead of their personal laptops. Internet access was also disabled to ensure that everyone solved the problem independently without using online resources or AI tools. The task was uploaded through Google Classroom, and we were given forty minutes to complete it. When I first read the problem, I felt some pressure because open-ended tasks require both problem-solving and programming skills. However, instead of rushing, I followed a structured approach. First, I carefully analyzed the problem, then I identified possible solutions, and finally selected the most efficient approach before starting the implementation. This strategy helped me stay focused and avoid unnecessary mistakes. Thanks to my strong preparation throughout the semester and consistent practice in previous labs, I was able to complete the task successfully within the given time. After submission, our instructor reviewed the work, and Alhamdulillah, I secured full marks in the evaluation. This achievement was another reminder that regular practice and understanding concepts deeply often produce better results than last-minute preparation.

At the same time, our Communication Skills course was also reaching its practical conclusion. Since we had already completed the theoretical portion before the midterm examinations, the remaining weeks focused entirely on presentations and performance-based activities. For our final assignment, students were required to form groups of three and select one topic from the communication concepts we had studied during the semester. My DLD project teammates and I decided to remain in the same group and selected Types of Communication as our topic. We wanted our presentation to be different from the others, so instead of only explaining the concepts through slides, we designed an interactive demonstration. First, we introduced communication and explained the concepts of verbal and non-verbal communication through a presentation. Then we created a practical scenario where three friends met in a playground and discussed the recent Pakistan vs. Bangladesh cricket series. This conversation represented verbal communication because information was being exchanged through spoken words. After that, we shifted to non-verbal communication by acting out a cricket match in front of the class. One group member became the bowler, another became the batsman, and the third acted as the umpire. The umpire communicated decisions such as fours, sixes and not-outs using gestures and signals, while the class was asked to interpret their meanings. This activity made the concepts easy to understand and kept the audience engaged throughout the session. Alhamdulillah, our presentation was highly appreciated by both our classmates and instructor, and we achieved 10 out of 10 marks. Looking back, this week was filled with academic achievements, practical learning experiences, teamwork and creativity, making it one of the most memorable periods of my second semester.
