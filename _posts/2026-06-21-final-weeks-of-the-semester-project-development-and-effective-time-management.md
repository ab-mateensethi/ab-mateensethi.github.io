As the last two weeks of our second semester began, the academic workload increased significantly. Most subjects were close to completing their syllabus and several important submissions and projects had to be finalized before the semester ended. One of the major tasks was the submission of our **Database Systems Lab Manual**. Since Friday was an off day at our university, our database labs were conducted online and our instructor had initially given us a deadline to complete and submit all fourteen labs by Sunday. We were required to complete both the assigned and any pending lab tasks, then submit them in both soft and hard copy formats. At first, this created pressure among many students because preparing all fourteen labs together while managing other subjects was challenging. However, after students requested additional time, our instructor kindly extended the deadline. This allowed us to organize our work more effectively. My classmates and I worked consistently, completed all labs from Lab 1 to Lab 14, submitted the soft copies online and later submitted the spiral-bound hard copies as required. Completing this task gave us a sense of relief and allowed us to focus on other important academic responsibilities.

Alongside the database lab submission, we also had pending work in our **Object-Oriented Programming (OOP) Lab**. One open-ended lab activity was still remaining and we were simultaneously managing two major projects. The first project involved designing a console-based university management system using a three-tier architecture, which simulated the type of record management used in Learning Management Systems. The second project was particularly exciting because it required us to develop a game using **C#**. Students were allowed to create the game either with or without a framework. Those who used a framework were encouraged to work with **Unity**, while others could develop their projects using Windows Forms. Since I had always been interested in practical software development, I was excited about this opportunity. Although I already had some experience with Windows Forms through our OOP theory classes taught by Dr. Akram, I wanted to challenge myself by learning something new. Therefore, I selected Unity as my development platform. With guidance from our instructor and the help of online tutorials and learning resources, I began working on my project. After brainstorming different ideas, I decided to create an **Endless Car Lane Runner** game. My goal was to design a game that was simple, user-friendly and visually attractive. Since it was my first game development project, I focused on learning the fundamentals while giving my best effort. Although Unity presented several challenges and technical errors during development, I continued working on the project every day, gradually improving both the game and my skills.

While managing these academic responsibilities, another challenge appeared during the weekend. Since the semester was approaching its end, many hostel students returned to their homes, which significantly reduced the number of students remaining on campus. As a result, the hostel management officially announced that the mess facility would remain closed during the weekend due to the low number of residents. This meant that the remaining students had to arrange their own meals from the university canteen or nearby restaurants. Since my friends and I were already busy with projects, assignments and other academic work, regularly going outside for meals was not always convenient. To manage our time efficiently, we adopted a flexible routine. Sometimes we ate at the university canteen, while on other occasions we visited nearby restaurants, especially during the evenings. Despite these adjustments, we ensured that our academic progress continued without interruption. One important lesson I had learned from my first semester was the importance of staying ahead of deadlines. During my first semester, being new to university life had caused me to face pressure near the end of the term. However, by the second semester, I had developed better planning and time management skills. As a result, I was able to balance projects, submissions, daily responsibilities and personal challenges more effectively. Looking back, these final weeks of the semester taught me valuable lessons about discipline, adaptability and the importance of managing multiple responsibilities simultaneously.
