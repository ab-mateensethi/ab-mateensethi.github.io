As my third semester began, university life gradually returned to its regular routine and I continued working towards my academic and personal development goals. Alongside my university studies, I was also completing a personal project that I had started during my summer holidays: Lingua AI, an AI-powered language translation web application. The main purpose of this project was to make translating text between different languages simple, fast and convenient for students, freelancers and other users who communicate in multiple languages. I wanted to create something practical that could solve a real-world problem while helping me strengthen the programming and technical skills I had developed during my first two semesters. During my holidays, I had completed different Coursera courses and learned new concepts and now I wanted to apply that knowledge by building a working application. I developed the backend using Python and used libraries and translation services to convert text from one language into another. For the user interface, I used Streamlit, which allowed me to design and deploy a responsive web application without building a separate frontend from scratch. Lingua AI supports more than 17 languages, including English, Urdu, German, French, Chinese, Japanese and Italian, allowing users to select their source and destination languages and receive translations conveniently. I also added useful features such as text-to-speech playback, a one-click copy option and translation history so that users could listen to translated text, copy results easily and revisit previous translations. To improve reliability, I combined Google Translate with a backup translation engine, helping the application remain useful when the primary translation service was unavailable or unreliable. Completing this project gave me practical experience with Python, Streamlit, API integration, user-interface design and the process of turning an idea into a working web application. Although Lingua AI was not an official university assignment, it was an independent learning project that I created to improve my practical abilities and explore my interest in Artificial Intelligence and Natural Language Processing. After starting it during the holidays, I brought it close to completion as my university routine resumed and eventually completed it. I then shared the project on my GitHub profile and LinkedIn so that other people could explore it and benefit from it. You can try the live application and explore its source code through the following links:

- Live Application: [Try Lingua AI](https://lingua-ai-translate.streamlit.app/)
- Source Code: [Explore the GitHub Repository](https://github.com/ab-mateensethi/LanguageTranslationTool)

I also shared screenshots of the application's interface to show how it works. For me, completing Lingua AI was a meaningful achievement because it allowed me to move beyond simply studying programming concepts and start applying them to a practical product. It strengthened my confidence and motivated me to continue building projects, whether they are academic assignments or independent initiatives. With this project completed, I continued following the same approach in my third semester: attending lectures regularly, revising what I learned, completing daily tasks on time and developing a strong understanding of every subject from the beginning.

Whenever I returned to my hostel after classes, I revised the day's lectures and completed any written work or assignments. I believed that keeping up with daily studies would make the upcoming quizzes, tests and examinations easier to manage. During the second and third weeks, our regular classes and laboratory sessions gradually became more organized and we began working in groups for our practical activities. Two particularly interesting laboratory subjects for me were Data Structures and Algorithms (DSA) and Computer Networks. In DSA, we studied concepts such as stacks and linked lists in theory and then implemented them through programming during our laboratory sessions. This helped me understand how these data structures work in practice rather than memorizing their definitions alone. Computer Networks also became one of my favourite subjects. Initially, I thought it might be mostly theoretical, but the laboratory sessions changed my perspective. We studied protocols and networking concepts such as HTTP, TCP, UDP and IP and then used Wireshark to observe and analyze network traffic and protocols in practice. Seeing the concepts from our lectures in actual network packets made the subject more interesting and helped me connect theory with real-world applications. Meanwhile, our teachers began announcing quizzes for the coming weeks, with some planning assessments for Week Four and others for Week Five. I continued managing my academic responsibilities while also participating in university activities. During the third week, our departmental sports league began and I took an active role in organizing and managing our class cricket team. As the Event Management Head of IEEE CIS, I already had society responsibilities, but I also wanted to represent my class and encourage students to participate in extracurricular activities. We visited the CE-26 classes, collected the names of students interested in playing and helped arrange trials to form the teams. The departmental league included several sports, such as cricket, football, volleyball and indoor games. I helped manage our cricket team while continuing my academic routine. Participating in sports gave me a refreshing break from studies, helped me stay connected with my classmates and reminded me that university development is not limited to academics alone. Managing studies, projects, sports and society responsibilities together required planning and consistency, so I continued trying to complete my daily academic work before moving on to other activities.

As the fourth week approached, another important responsibility arrived: the induction process for new members of the IEEE Computational Intelligence Society (IEEE CIS) team. Since I had joined the society as its Event Management Head, I was now involved in preparing for the selection of new students who wanted to become part of the team. We held two or three meetings with our existing CIS team and received guidance from our seniors, including discussions about the interview process, possible questions, responsibilities and the most suitable date for the inductions. We considered different suggestions and worked together to plan the process properly. To communicate with students more effectively, I also created a WhatsApp community that brought together students from both sections of CE-26 and both sections of CE-25. My aim was to establish a common online platform where students could receive departmental updates without requiring us to visit classrooms repeatedly and potentially disturb ongoing lectures. Within the community, I created separate groups for different purposes, including the Welcome Party, CE-26 Study Hub, Departmental Seminars and Sports Events. This structure helped us organize communication and share relevant information with students more efficiently. We used the community to distribute the CIS registration form, induction posters and details about the interview date, time and venue. As a result, many students learned about the opportunity and several showed interest by registering for the selection process. When the induction day arrived, our CIS Chairperson assigned responsibilities to the team members and my name was included among those responsible for managing the interview arrangements. As Event Management Head, my duties included maintaining discipline among the students waiting for their interviews and helping ensure that the process continued smoothly. I also participated in conducting interviews alongside the assigned panel members. We asked students why they wanted to join the society, what motivated them and how they believed they could contribute to the team. This was a particularly valuable experience for me because it was one of my first opportunities to help conduct interviews and interact with juniors in a formal selection setting. I learned more about professional communication, teamwork, consistency and the importance of creating an organized environment in which every candidate could be assessed fairly. My earlier experiences, including attending professional seminars and helping manage the STLC event, had already taught me useful lessons, but this responsibility gave me another opportunity to apply those lessons in practice. After the interviews, our team evaluated the candidates based on several factors, including confidence, communication skills, previous activities and experience in student societies. We assigned points according to our evaluation criteria and planned to discuss the results collectively before finalizing the new team. At the time, the final selection had not yet been announced. Our Chairperson explained that a further meeting would be held with the relevant IEEE leadership before the IEEE CIS team for 2026 was finalized. While waiting for the outcome, I continued managing my regular classes, practical assignments, personal projects and other responsibilities. Looking back at these first weeks of my third semester, I realized that I was applying the lessons from my first year in increasingly practical ways. Completing Lingua AI helped me strengthen my technical skills, regular study habits supported my academic progress, laboratory work improved my understanding of computer engineering concepts, sports kept me connected with my classmates and IEEE CIS gave me opportunities to develop leadership and event-management experience. I was learning that progress comes from consistently combining knowledge with action. With the guidance of Allah, I hoped to continue improving myself, contribute positively to my department and society and make the rest of my third semester productive, meaningful and full of new learning opportunities.
