As my summer holidays continued to be both enjoyable and productive, another important opportunity came my way through my university. During these holidays, registrations opened for IEEE at our university, including the IEEE student activities and the computing-focused society for students interested in computer and computational technologies. IEEE stands for the **Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers**, a global professional organization that works to advance technology, engineering, education, innovation, and professional development. Within the IEEE community, computing-focused groups provide opportunities for students and professionals to learn, collaborate, develop technical skills, and contribute to the technology community. When I first entered university in my first semester, IEEE registrations had also opened, but at that time I was completely new to university life. I did not have enough knowledge about these societies, their purpose, or the opportunities they could provide, so I did not register. However, after completing my first two semesters, my perspective had changed. During my holidays, I was already in contact with some of my seniors, and I had previously told them that I wanted to become part of IEEE when I got another opportunity. When the registrations opened again, I contacted my seniors and expressed my interest in joining the computing-focused IEEE society. After registering, I was informed that an online interview would be conducted for the students who had applied. The interview was an important part of the selection process because students were being considered for different responsibilities and positions within the society. Alhamdulillah, I appeared in the interview with confidence. I was asked questions about why I wanted to join IEEE, how I worked in a team, and how I could contribute to the society. I explained that I already had experience managing the official Computer Engineering social media pages and communicating with students through them. During the holidays, juniors from BCE-26 had also contacted me through these official pages, and guiding and communicating with them helped me improve my communication skills. I also shared my previous experience of bringing classmates together during exhibitions and helping create a platform where students could work collectively. These experiences gave me confidence while answering the interview questions. Alhamdulillah, exactly one week after the interviews, the results were announced, and I received the position of **Event Management Head** in the IEEE CIS team. This was a very proud and happy moment for me because it was not only a new position but also a new responsibility. I felt grateful to Allah for giving me this opportunity, and I was determined to use this role positively, contribute to my team, support my department, and give my best to the society in the future.

After receiving this responsibility, my holidays became even more meaningful because I was not only learning individually but also becoming involved in a professional student community. As I had already decided, I wanted my holidays to be a combination of enjoyment, learning, and personal development, and my involvement with IEEE helped me continue in the same direction. During the holidays, different induction activities, online sessions, and seminars were organized for students. One of the seminars I attended was **“Building a Standout Professional Identity.”** Before attending it, we completed the online registration, and later the seminar was conducted online through Google Classroom. The session focused on professional development and helped us understand how we could build a stronger professional identity as students. One of the most useful parts for me was learning how to create and improve a professional LinkedIn profile. They explained how students can present their education, skills, projects, achievements, and interests professionally so that their online presence can support their future career opportunities. The seminar also introduced us to different aspects of professionalism and ended with a question-and-answer session, which made it more interactive and useful. After that, I also attended another online seminar called **“Mastering GitHub.”** Although I had already been using GitHub during my first two semesters and had worked on projects and repositories, this seminar helped me understand GitHub at a deeper and more professional level. The speakers explained many things from the basics to more advanced practical concepts, including how to make a GitHub account look professional, how repositories work, how projects can be cloned, and how students can organize and present their work effectively. This was especially useful for me because I already had my own GitHub portfolio and had been using GitHub for my university projects. Therefore, I did not attend these seminars only to collect certificates; I genuinely tried to learn something from each session and then apply that knowledge in my own work. I believe this is one of the most valuable parts of attending such activities: a certificate is useful, but the real benefit comes when the knowledge learned from the session creates an actual improvement in your skills and professional profile. In this way, my holidays continued to move forward with a balance of learning, leadership, and personal development, while I also remained connected with my university community.

As the holidays gradually started coming to an end, I realized that only around two weeks were left before the university was going to reopen for the third semester. At this stage, I started reconnecting with my university friends and asking them how their holidays had gone and what they had been doing during the break. These conversations made me feel that university life was approaching again, and I also started preparing myself mentally and practically for the new semester. I became busy with shopping and preparing the things I would need for university, including clothes and other necessary items. During these last two weeks, I went to the market with my parents to complete my shopping and make sure that everything was ready before leaving for university. I wanted to finish these preparations in advance so that I could return with a relaxed mind instead of worrying about unfinished tasks at the last moment. At the same time, I was genuinely excited because, after a long break, I was finally going to return to university, meet my friends again, and experience the beginning of another chapter of my university journey. I was also looking forward to meeting the new juniors who would be joining the campus and interacting with them, especially because I had already experienced how meaningful communication and guidance can be for students who are new to university life. This time, however, my return had another special meaning for me because I was not going back only as a student; I was also returning with a new responsibility as the **Event Management Head of the IEEE CIS team**. I was excited about representing my society, contributing to its activities, working with my team, and putting the leadership and communication experience I had gained during my first two semesters into practice. Looking back at the beginning of my holidays, I had wanted to make this break both enjoyable and progressive, and now, as it was coming to an end, I felt that I had achieved that balance in many ways. I had worked on my health, celebrated my academic achievement, continued learning through Coursera and professional seminars, improved my technical and professional skills, and received a new leadership opportunity through IEEE. With my shopping completed, my luggage gradually getting ready, and my mind prepared for a new semester, I was excited to begin my **third semester with a fresh mindset, new responsibilities, and new goals**. Most importantly, I was thankful to Allah for every opportunity and achievement and prayed that He would guide me, give me strength, and help me perform my responsibilities sincerely and successfully in the journey ahead.
